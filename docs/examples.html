Templates & Examples

A closer look at the logs, journals, and templates that help turn small daily actions into meaningful long-term reflection.

1. Yearly Logs

Each year provides a ready-to-use structure for recording activities, learning, and progress.

Yearly Structure

logs/
└── 2027/
    ├── README.md
    ├── calendar.md
    ├── daily/
    ├── weekly/
    └── monthly/

Each component serves a different purpose:

  • README.md: Set intentions at the beginning of the year and reflect at the end.
  • calendar.md: Maintain a visual overview of your year.
  • daily/: Record contributions and daily activities.
  • weekly/: Review progress and challenges.
  • monthly/: Identify patterns and reflect on direction.

2. Your Year at a Glance

The yearly calendar provides a visual record of your journey. It is more than a contribution tracker: it captures different aspects of your growth.

Calendar Indicators

  • 🌱 TO-BE
  • ✅ Showing up / making progress
  • 💻 Technical work
  • 📚 Learning
  • ⭐ Major events or milestones
  • 🔥 Busy periods
  • 🚀 Focused work periods
  • ✨ Memorable moments

Use the indicators to capture what made a particular day or period meaningful.

4. Reusable Templates

Templates provide lightweight starting points so you don't have to create a format every time.

Daily Log

Capture what you worked on, what you learned, and what comes next.

Weekly Reflection

Review your contributions, challenges, and progress.

Monthly Reflection

Look for patterns, recognize milestones, and consider future direction.

Project Log

Document project goals, development, decisions, and lessons learned.

Experiment Log

Record ideas, experiments, observations, and outcomes.

5. Putting Everything Together

These components are designed to complement one another rather than create more work.

  1. Show up: Make a small contribution or meaningful progress.
  2. Log: Record what actually happened.
  3. Reflect: Review your experiences at different intervals.
  4. Explore: Use journals to think about your longer-term direction.
  5. Grow: Apply what you've learned to your next steps.

You don't need to use every template or complete every journal. Start with what is useful and let your system evolve naturally.

Make the System Your Own.

Take what works, adapt what doesn't, and build a practice that supports your journey.
